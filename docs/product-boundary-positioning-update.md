# Product boundary and positioning update (nationwide analysis-only posture)

> **Status:** Synthesis of `docs/nationwide-legal-regulatory-posture-brief.md` and `docs/competitor-credit-positioning-brief.md`. This is a product-boundary recommendation, not legal advice.

## Why this update exists

Two new findings now need to shape the product boundary:

1. **Nationwide legality is not yet established** merely because the product only analyzes consumer-uploaded reports and does not handle disputes.
2. **Visible market comparators often lead with repair/dispute/score-improvement positioning**, which is exactly the posture this product should avoid if it wants to preserve an analysis-only educational boundary.

## Updated posture

### What we can say confidently
- The intended product is **U.S.-only** and **consumer-facing**.
- The intended product is **educational** and **analysis-only**.
- The intended product accepts only a **consumer-uploaded report the consumer is authorized to use**.
- The intended product returns output only to that **same authenticated consumer**.
- The intended product does **not** handle disputes, contact bureaus/furnishers, or issue legal verdicts.

### What we should **not** say yet
- That the product is clearly lawful in **all 50 states**.
- That analysis-only automatically places the product outside all credit-repair / credit-services / licensing regimes.
- That a **paid** nationwide launch is already supportable.

## Recommended launch boundary

Until further state-level review or counsel clearance, the recommended boundary remains:
- consumer-upload only,
- consumer-only delivery,
- no third-party sharing,
- no underwriting or eligibility use,
- no dispute generation,
- no bureau/furnisher/creditor contact,
- no legal conclusions,
- no score-improvement promises,
- no deletion/removal promises,
- free pilot posture preferred over paid launch,
- strict minimization / deletion / safeguards posture.

## Positioning guidance

## Say this kind of thing
Prefer language like:
- analyze your report
- review your report
- understand what your report shows
- educational explanation of reported data
- identify inconsistencies for your review
- highlight bureau differences
- summarize what appears on the report
- questions to investigate yourself
- understand your rights and next steps

## Avoid this kind of thing
Do not use language like:
- credit repair
- fix your credit
- clean up your credit
- remove negative items
- delete items
- boost your score
- improve your score
- get approved faster
- challenge bureaus on your behalf
- creditor intervention
- legal strategy
- guaranteed results

## UX / product implications

### Onboarding
Onboarding should frame the service as:
- report review,
- educational explanation,
- consumer-controlled interpretation,
- no representation and no bureau action.

It should **not** look like:
- a repair intake,
- a dispute intake,
- an advocacy engagement,
- a score-optimization funnel.

### Output report
The output should emphasize:
- findings with provenance,
- explanations of what each field means,
- bureau-to-bureau comparison,
- educational context,
- cautious next-step categories.

The output should **not** include:
- dispute letters,
- recommended creditor/bureau contact scripts framed as representation,
- legal conclusions,
- removal promises,
- personalized claims of entitlement.

### Pricing
The current safest posture remains:
- genuinely free pilot first,
- no recurring repair-service framing,
- no payment path until the paid-model legal boundary is better supported.

## Nationwide rollout implication

A nationwide consumer product may still be the long-term goal, but the current research supports treating that as an **open rollout question**, not a settled assumption.

Recommended stance:
- **Product vision:** U.S.-only nationwide ambition.
- **Current compliance posture:** nationwide not yet validated.
- **Operational implication:** be prepared to launch only after additional state-law review, or to limit launch scope if required.

## Recommended next decisions

1. Preserve the **analysis-only educational** boundary in all product copy and UI.
2. Treat **paid launch** as a separate legal/commercial step, not a default continuation of the free pilot.
3. Add a future work item for a **state-law scan / counsel review** before claiming nationwide paid availability.
4. Keep the repo's current output guard and consider expanding copy review to all marketing / UX surfaces.

## Plain-English conclusion

The right framing is **not**:
> "Other credit-repair companies offer analysis, so we can too."

The stronger framing is:
> "Many visible competitors are actually repair/dispute businesses. If this product wants the lower-risk analysis-only posture, it needs to look visibly different from them in copy, workflow, promises, and pricing."
