# State mini memo: Tennessee

> **Status:** Preliminary state memo from regulator materials. Not legal advice.

## Question
Does Tennessee have a recent explicit licensing regime for adjacent debt/credit-help activity that should make us cautious even if this product remains analysis-only?

## What surfaced
Tennessee announced that starting Jan. 1, 2026, individuals offering **debt resolution services** to Tennessee consumers must first obtain a **license** through the Tennessee Department of Commerce & Insurance.

The announcement describes a new statutory framework governing traditional debt settlement services and includes licensing, disclosures, fee limits, and prohibited-practice signals.

## What this means for this product
### Verified
- Tennessee has a recent, explicit licensing framework for adjacent debt-resolution activity.
- This is not the same thing as an analysis-only report-review product.

### Best current inference
Even though this platform is not intended to negotiate or settle debts, Tennessee is a reminder that adjacent debt/credit-assistance categories can become explicitly licensed. Any drift toward negotiating, counseling around settlement, or acting as an intermediary would worsen risk.

## Practical conclusion
- Tennessee is a **priority review state**.
- Analysis-only posture matters here.
- Do not assume adjacent licensing regimes are irrelevant just because the current product does not handle disputes.
