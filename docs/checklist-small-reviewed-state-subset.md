# Checklist: small reviewed-state subset

> **Status:** Execution checklist for the weaker multi-state pilot option. This is not legal advice.

## Goal
Run a **free, invite-only, analysis-only pilot** in a **very small reviewed-state subset** without implying general nationwide availability.

## 1. Scope decision
- [ ] Name each reviewed launch state explicitly.
- [ ] Record why each state was included.
- [ ] Record why excluded states remain out of scope.
- [ ] Confirm this is still a pilot, not a general U.S. launch.

## 2. State-by-state eligibility logic
- [ ] Add an explicit approved-state list to the governance model.
- [ ] Require state-of-residence confirmation before upload.
- [ ] Block unsupported states from entering the pilot flow.
- [ ] Define support escalation rules for mixed-state edge cases.

## 3. Product boundary
- [ ] Keep the product analysis-only and educational.
- [ ] Keep the product consumer-uploaded and consumer-only.
- [ ] Keep disputes, bureau contact, and legal conclusions out of scope.
- [ ] Keep the pilot genuinely free.

## 4. Copy and positioning
- [ ] Apply `docs/copy-boundary-guide.md` across all surfaces.
- [ ] State clearly that availability is limited to approved pilot states.
- [ ] Avoid broad U.S. availability language.
- [ ] Avoid repair/dispute/score-improvement positioning.

## 5. Governance / approval burden
- [ ] Confirm the accountable owners are comfortable with a multi-state pilot posture.
- [ ] Ensure the legal/privacy/security review explicitly addresses the state-subset approach.
- [ ] Maintain evidence linking each included state to its review rationale.

## 6. Operational caution
- [ ] Verify that state gating is testable and supportable.
- [ ] Verify that content/copy can remain neutral across the included states.
- [ ] Verify that expansion pressure does not silently turn this into a nationwide claim.

## Completion condition
This checklist is complete only when:
- the approved-state subset is explicitly recorded,
- unsupported states are blocked,
- the pilot remains free and analysis-only,
- and all reviewers are comfortable with the added complexity.
