# 50-state review tracker: analysis-only consumer credit-report platform

> **Status:** Preliminary state-law research tracker based on Exa-backed `.gov` research plus selected direct regulator-page captures. This is **not legal advice** and **not a 50-state clearance memo**. A state marked "no clear state-specific signal surfaced in preliminary search" is **not approved** and **not cleared**; it only means the first-pass search did not surface an obvious state-specific credit-services / licensing result.

## How to read this tracker

Categories used here:
- **Explicit regime surfaced** — preliminary research found a state regulator, statute, registration, licensing, or analogous formal framework that is directly relevant to credit services / debt settlement / similar consumer-credit assistance activity.
- **General consumer-protection signal only** — preliminary research found enforcement or general consumer-protection materials, but not a clean state-specific licensing/registration answer for this exact product shape.
- **No clear state-specific signal surfaced in preliminary search** — preliminary Exa search did not surface an obvious state-specific regulator page or statute for the searched terms. This is **not clearance**.

## High-level conclusion

### What this research supports
- It does **not** support claiming the product is already clearly allowed in all 50 states.
- It does support treating a nationwide launch as a **state-by-state review problem**, especially once money is charged or product language drifts toward credit repair / improvement.
- It strongly supports keeping the product boundary narrow: analysis-only, educational, consumer-uploaded, consumer-only, no disputes, no bureau/furnisher contact, no score-improvement promises.

### Strong-signal states surfaced directly in this pass
These states surfaced explicit regimes or strong analogs and should be treated as **priority review states**:
- **California** — DFPI oversight / registration framework for debt settlement services under the CCFPL.
- **Maryland** — explicit Credit Services Business licensing page.

- **Wisconsin** — explicit statutory subchapter for Credit Services Organizations.
- **Tennessee** — explicit 2026 debt-resolution licensing framework.

- **Vermont** — regulator study signal showing active state attention to regulatory models for credit-repair/consumer-protection issues.

## Anchored examples from direct captures

### Maryland — explicit regime surfaced
Maryland's Office of Financial Regulation states that a **Credit Services Business** is a business that, for payment, sells/provides/represents it can provide services to improve a consumer's credit record/history/rating, establish a new credit file, and/or obtain an extension of credit; Maryland states a **license is required**.  
Source: `https://www.labor.maryland.gov/finance/industry/creditserv.shtml`

### Utah — explicit regime surfaced
Utah's Division of Consumer Protection states a credit services organization and related sellers/agents/representatives may not conduct business regulated by the Credit Services Organizations Act in Utah without first securing a **certificate of registration** and the **required surety**.  
Source: `https://commerce.utah.gov/dcp/for-businesses/credit-service-organizations/`

### Wisconsin — explicit regime surfaced
Wisconsin search surfaced **Subchapter V Credit Services Organizations** with registration requirements and prohibited activities.  
Source surfaced: `https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/document/statutes/422.pdf`

### Tennessee — explicit analogous regime surfaced
Tennessee announced that starting Jan. 1, 2026, individuals offering **debt resolution services** to Tennessee consumers must first obtain a **license** from the Tennessee Department of Commerce & Insurance. This is not the same as an analysis-only product, but it is a strong signal that adjacent credit/debt-help activity is explicitly regulated.  
Source: `https://www.tn.gov/commerce/news/2026/1/5/new-licensing-requirements-consumer-protections-for-tn-consumers-through-debt-resolution-services-act.html`

### California — explicit adjacent regime surfaced
California DFPI states that effective Feb. 15, 2025, no person may provide **debt settlement services** to California residents without first registering with DFPI under the CCFPL. Again, this is not the same as pure analysis-only, but it confirms California has active oversight of adjacent consumer-credit assistance categories.  
Source: `https://dfpi.ca.gov/regulated-industries/covered-persons/debt-settlement-services/`

### Texas — explicit signal surfaced, but still needs cleaner follow-up
Texas search surfaced state materials referencing **Credit Services Organization** / related registration signals through state regulator pages. Preliminary result is enough to treat Texas as a priority review state, but a cleaner state-specific memo is still needed before relying on this as a final classification answer.  
Source surfaced: `https://occc.texas.gov/alecs_faq/`

### Vermont — active study signal surfaced
Vermont search surfaced a Department of Financial Regulation report studying regulatory models for protections and remedies, which is not a direct licensing page but is a signal that the state is actively considering this space.  
Source surfaced: `https://legislature.vermont.gov/assets/Legislative-Reports/DFR-Act-23-of-2025-Section-18-Report.pdf`

## What this means operationally

### Do not claim yet
Do **not** claim:
- available in all 50 states,
- cleared nationwide,
- outside all credit-services or licensing laws,
- ready for paid nationwide launch.

### Safer current claim
A safer current claim is:
- U.S.-only product vision,
- current analysis-only educational boundary,
- state-law rollout and paid-model clearance remain open,
- nationwide availability is a goal, not a validated current compliance claim.

## Recommended next state-law work

1. Treat these as **priority follow-up states**: California, Maryland, Utah, Wisconsin, Tennessee, Texas, Vermont, and likely New York/Florida even though they did not surface a clean result in the first pass.
2. For each priority state, prepare a one-state mini memo answering:
   - relevant statute/regulator,
   - whether analysis-only appears inside or outside the regime,
   - whether payment changes the answer,
   - what language/features most increase risk,
   - whether registration/licensing/bonding is implicated.
3. First-round and follow-up memo indexes now exist at `docs/priority-state-memo-index.md` and `docs/followup-state-memo-index.md`; treat them as research inputs, not final clearance.
4. Do not move to a paid nationwide claim until those mini memos are expanded or counsel completes a real 50-state review.
